04 // AI & PHYSICS ENGINE

Mathematical Core:
Density Clustering & Kinematic Plume Attribution

Moving beyond static thresholding. VAYU-NET couples spatial statistics with atmospheric kinematics to trace pollution to its physical origin.

UNSUPERVISED LEARNING
1. DBSCAN Hotspot Clustering

Density-Based Spatial Clustering of Applications with Noise (DBSCAN) using the Great-Circle Haversine distance metric.

$D_{haversine}(p_1, p_2) \le \epsilon$
$\text{Density Condition: } |N_\epsilon(p)| \ge \text{MinPts}$

Automatically detects multi-point clusters (stubble burning belts, industrial zones) while isolating spurious sensor glitches as statistical noise.

ATMOSPHERIC PHYSICS
2. Upwind Trajectory Corridors

Decomposes synoptic 10m wind velocity into orthogonal directional vectors to construct backward transport trajectories.

$u = -v_{wind} \cdot \sin(\theta_{heading})$
$v = -v_{wind} \cdot \cos(\theta_{heading})$

Projects backward corridor cones tracing where air masses travelled 3 to 12 hours prior to reaching urban ground reference stations.

METEOROLOGICAL INVERSION
3. Inversion Trapping Dynamics

Quantifies vertical atmospheric compression using Boundary Layer Height (BLH) and thermal lapse rates.

$\text{Trapping Factor } \kappa = \frac{BLH_{standard}}{BLH_{observed}}$
$\text{Severe Trapping if } BLH < 300\text{m}$

Distinguishes between genuine emission spikes and artificial smog accumulation caused by nocturnal winter thermal inversions.

Automated Kinematic Decision Tree
Rules-assisted ML classifier categorizes sources: Biomass Burning (Satellite fire overlap + upwind heading) vs Road Dust & Traffic (High PM10/PM2.5 ratio) vs Thermal Inversion Trapping (Low wind + collapsed BLH).
Evidence-Based Attribution
Mathematical Modeling, Physics Trajectories & Source Classification
